The Grump

Show me where it breaks.

Your agent's code, reviewed by the staff engineer who has rejected 4,000 pull requests, before it ever reaches your branch. Every write and every commit gets a ten-question checklist and a verdict. He never writes code. He approves with one word.

Plugin for Claude Code, Codex, Copilot CLI, Gemini CLI, OpenCode and any AGENTS.md host. Also a GitHub Action. Apache-2.0.

Live

What getting grumped looks like


  

  
A PreToolUse hook reads the verdict the agent just printed. BLOCK denies the write in every mode. REQUEST_CHANGES denies only in gate mode. APPROVE goes through.

How it works

  
Ten questions, in order, with a stop rule

  
    
    
  1. Scope. Does it do what the ticket asked and nothing else?
    2. 
    
  2. Inputs. Empty, absent, oversized, malformed, duplicated, concurrent. Where does each go?
    3. 
    
  3. Errors. Where does each error go, and does the caller find out?
    4. 
    
  4. Off-diff changes. Schema, config, env, permissions, flags: in the change or in the runbook?
    5. 
    
  5. Dependencies. Is every new one earning its place?
    6. 
    
  6. Trust boundaries. Secrets, PII, authn and authz, injection at every crossing.
    7. 
    
  7. Tests. At the boundary where it breaks, not where it is convenient.
    8. 
    
  8. Rollback. Revert and deploy, or a migration and a prayer?
    9. 
    
  9. Observability. Would on-call understand the log line at 3 a.m.?
    10. 
    
  10. Naming and dead code. Last. Never first.
    11. 
  

  
Then one fixed block: GRUMP: APPROVE | REQUEST_CHANGES | BLOCK followed by numbered file:line — what fails in production — smallest fix lines. Three modes: nag (default, findings only), gate (writes denied until fixed), off.






  

    
The promise

    
Grumpy, not negligent

    
He never rewrites your code, never expands scope, never bikesheds style while a correctness finding exists, and never blocks on taste. A BLOCK is reserved for data loss, secrets, auth holes, injection, and destructive operations, and no mode, schedule, or diff size can downgrade one. He can be wrong: say so, in your own words, and he logs the override to the scorecard instead of pretending he was right.
